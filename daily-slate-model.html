BaseRuns and Log 5 are implemented exactly as specified, with no change to their coefficients. Total bases allowed are reconstructed from hits, doubles, triples and home runs surrendered, since the pitching side does not publish the figure. Caught stealing and grounded-into-double-play totals are frequently absent from the pitching feed. Treating them as zero would understate outs on the defensive side only and systematically inflate BaseRuns allowed, dragging every team's rating down by roughly eight points of win percentage; since no team allows zero of either across a season, a zero there is read as missing and estimated from at-bats at league-typical rates so both sides stay symmetric. Values actually supplied are never overwritten, and the estimate is skipped below 500 at-bats where a true zero is plausible. Log 5 here produces a full-game probability from season-long team quality; it does not account for the starting pitchers in a given game, so it is a baseline rather than a game-specific price, and a heavy mismatch of starters can legitimately move a real line well away from it. The sequencing flag compares actual runs to BaseRuns across the season to date: it identifies teams whose results have diverged from their underlying performance, which is a statement about the past and a tendency going forward, not a projection for today's game. Fair odds are the model probability expressed in American terms with no margin applied, so any real market price will sit worse than fair. Team statistics come from the MLB Stats API.
