NBA PLAYER MODEL

Builds every game from the players who will actually be on the floor. Each player gets an offensive and a defensive rating fit from this season's box scores, team scoring and plus-minus, with last season as the starting point. Teams are the sum of their projected minutes, so trades, injuries and returning stars change the number immediately.

PLAYER RATINGS — team points = league base + home court + Σ (minutes/48 × offence) of its players − Σ (minutes/48 × defence) of the opponent's, fit on every team-game this season by ridge regression toward each player's box-score prior
LINEUPS — projected minutes from recent rotation, injured players removed and their minutes redistributed, editable per player
MATCHUPS — each player's counterpart, the opponent's defence by position (points, rebounds, assists and threes allowed per 48), and game pace
CHECK — walk-forward backtest against the closing lines ESPN stored for this season's games
SETTINGS
First run loads every box score this season (one request per game, cached in this browser, so later runs only load new games). Last season's player values come from the NBA Ensemble's training if you've run it; otherwise everyone starts from this season's box stats.
WALK-FORWARD BACKTEST
Every game after the warm-up is predicted using only games played before it, then graded against ESPN's stored closing spread and total. Lineups use the minutes actually played (the injury report usually tells you who sits), so treat this as a slightly optimistic test.

How the ratings work. For each team-game the model predicts points scored as a league base, plus half the home-court edge, minus a back-to-back term, plus the minute-weighted offensive ratings of that team's players, minus the minute-weighted defensive ratings of the opponents who played. Ratings are fit by ridge regression: each player is pulled toward a prior built from box stats (points, assists, rebounds, steals, blocks, turnovers per 36, and true shooting), blended with last season's production when the Ensemble has been trained. The prior strength setting is how many full games of evidence the prior is worth. A player's net rating is offence plus defence in points per 48 minutes above league average. Margin and total come straight from the two team point projections; win and cover chances use the spread of residuals measured in the backtest (defaults 12.5 and 17.5 before one is run). Props use per-minute production this season shrunk toward last season (or the position average), times projected minutes, times the opponent's allowed rate for that position, times game pace, with a small counterpart defence adjustment on points. No model sees late scratches, minutes limits or tanking before they're reported. This is a research tool, not betting advice.
